AI SEO Checklist: 30-Minute Pre-Publish Audit

Run these 24 checks on every new article before it goes live: eight timed sections, 30 minutes, one verdict.

1. Direct-answer opening

3 min00:00–03:00
  • 1.1B The first sentence answers the title's question.Pass: read alone, it's a full answer with its key number or condition.
  • 1.2The opening is two or three sentences and under about 60 words.Pass: count the words before the first paragraph break.
  • 1.3The answer states its scope: who, where or when it applies.Pass: any answer that changes by country, plan, size or date says which one.

2. Source attribution

4 min03:00–07:00
  • 2.1B Every number, quote and "research shows" claim links to its source where it's used.Pass: every highlighted number has a link in the same sentence.
  • 2.2Links go to the primary source, not a roundup that quotes it.Pass: each link lands on the study, the vendor's doc, the law or the dataset.
  • 2.3The source is named in the sentence, not only linked.Pass: no bare "here" or "this study" links.

3. Claim verification

6 min07:00–13:00
  • 3.1B Each linked number matches its source: same figure, unit, year and scope.Pass: every source opened and ticked. Any mismatch fixed or cut.
  • 3.2Claims with no source are cut, or rewritten as your own labelled view.Pass: no number, "study" or "expert" left without a link or a label.
  • 3.3Fast-moving facts carry a date.Pass: every price, statistic, feature, law and "best" claim has a month and year.

4. Entity clarity

4 min13:00–17:00
  • 4.1Each section names its subject in full, not "it", "we" or "the tool".Pass: the first sentence under every H2 names the subject.
  • 4.2Key terms are defined once ("X is a Y that Z"), and abbreviations are spelled out.Pass: the two or three core terms each have a definition sentence.
  • 4.3Article markup names the author, headline and dates, and matches the page.Pass: the Rich Results Test shows no errors, and the byline and dates match what readers see.

5. Semantic headings

3 min17:00–20:00
  • 5.1One H1 and no skipped heading levels.Pass: a heading checker shows one H1 and no jump from H2 to H4.
  • 5.2Each H2 and H3 names its topic or asks its sub-question.Pass: read alone, the headings say what each section answers. No "Overview" or "Wrapping up".
  • 5.3The first sentence under each heading answers that heading.Pass: spot-check every H2.

6. Tables and lists

3 min20:00–23:00
  • 6.1Comparisons of three or more items sit in an HTML table with a header row.Pass: no paragraph walks through "A costs this, B costs that, C costs the other."
  • 6.2Tables and charts are text, not screenshots.Pass: you can select the table's text on the preview page.
  • 6.3Steps are a numbered list, one action per item.Pass: every process with an order is numbered, and each step makes sense alone.

7. Crawl accessibility

4 min23:00–27:00
  • 7.1B The final URL returns a 200 status and its canonical tag points to itself.Pass: a status check shows 200, and the canonical is the post's own URL.
  • 7.2B The answer, tables and FAQ are in the HTML the server sends, not loaded by JavaScript or hidden in tabs.Pass: the raw HTML contains the opening, one table cell and one FAQ answer.
  • 7.3The post is linked from a live page and listed in the XML sitemap.Pass: a related post or the blog index links to it, and the sitemap lists the URL.

8. AI crawler directives

3 min27:00–30:00
  • 8.1B robots.txt allows Googlebot, Bingbot, OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot on the post's path.Pass: a robots.txt tester shows "allowed" for each.
  • 8.2B No unintended noindex, nosnippet, small max-snippet, noarchive or nocache rule in the page or its X-Robots-Tag header.Pass: the page source and headers show only rules you meant to set.
  • 8.3Your CDN or firewall lets verified search crawlers through.Pass: the search category is allowed, with no new rule since your last check.
