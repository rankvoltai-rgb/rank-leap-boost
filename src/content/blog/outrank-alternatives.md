## Why People Look for Outrank Alternatives

Outrank does a lot, and it keeps shipping. When people compare Outrank alternatives, their reasons are mostly about cost, depth, and measurement. These come up most.

### The $99 is where the bill starts

The All-in-One plan covers 30 articles, AI images, YouTube embeds, every integration, and the backlink exchange. Much of the rest is priced separately on Outrank's [pricing page](https://www.outrank.so/pricing). Premium Images cost $49 a month, 60 articles cost $85 more, 90 articles cost $160 more, and backlink plans run $199, $899, or $1,899 a month. Human editing is $1,399 a month, and directory submission is $500 once.

None of it is required. But 30 articles with premium images and the entry backlink plan come to $347 a month, by our arithmetic. Price the setup you'd actually use, not just the headline plan.

### Articles at 1,200 to 1,700 words

Outrank's [homepage](https://www.outrank.so/) promises a "minimum length of 1,200-1,700 words," and its [agencies page](https://www.outrank.so/for-agencies) says each article "ships at 1,200-1,700 words." That's plenty for many questions. For topics where the pages ranking now run to 3,000 words, you may want more depth.

Longer isn't automatically better, because length should follow what the searcher needs. Still, several Outrank alternatives aim longer: Rankbox writes about 2,750 words by default, SEObot averages about 3,000, and RankYak goes up to 5,000 when a topic calls for it.

### Backlinks depend on what you host

Outrank's included exchange pays you in credits for hosting other members' links, and Outrank says it verifies every placement. In [its words](https://www.outrank.so/backlink-building), "You earn them by letting Outrank place a relevant link in the articles it writes for you," and sites with a higher Domain Rating (DR) earn more per hosted link. There's no fixed number of links a month.

Faster link building is a separate purchase. The Backlink Scale Plan on Outrank's [backlinks page](https://www.outrank.so/backlinks) is $899 a month for 1,400 credits, and "A DR 60 blog costs 240 credits, a DR 30 one costs 120." By our arithmetic, that's about five DR 60 placements a month or 11 on DR 30 blogs, roughly $77 to $154 a link. Unspent credits roll over.

### No AI visibility tracking

Outrank's own [alternatives page](https://www.outrank.so/alternatives) calls one rival's AI visibility dashboard "a real extra we do not sell as a product." Outrank does offer a free AI Page Inspector that checks one URL, but not ongoing tracking. If you need to measure [AI visibility](/glossary/ai-visibility) over time, three Outrank alternatives here include some tracking: BabyLoveGrowth, Arvow, and RightBlogger.

Rankbox doesn't monitor citations yet either, so it won't fill that gap. To check by hand in the meantime, our free [AI Visibility Prompt Kit](/tools/ai-visibility-prompt-generator) gives you 30 buyer prompts to paste into ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini.

### Trial and billing terms

You can see keyword research and article generation before you pay, but "Trial articles include an Outrank watermark," and no trial length is stated. First-time subscribers can ask for a full refund within 7 days of the first payment, per Outrank's [terms](https://www.outrank.so/terms-of-use). That's more than several Outrank alternatives on this list offer.

On [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/outrank.so), Outrank had 3.6 out of 5 from 83 reviews when it was checked on 21 September 2026. Most reviews were five stars, and the low ones often mention cancelling and slow support replies.

## A Word on Backlink Exchanges and Paid Links

Outrank's included exchange trades links between members, and so do most Outrank alternatives on this list. Rankbox's exchange is rolling out on paid plans too. Exchanges are popular because links are hard to earn, but they share a risk: Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) list "excessive link exchanges" as link spam.

Paid links have their own rule. The same policies treat buying links for ranking purposes as link spam unless the links are marked rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow". Several tools here sell link plans on top of the base subscription, including Outrank's $199 to $1,899 tiers and the credit packs other tools sell. Before you buy any of them, ask how the placements are marked, and weigh the risk against what the links are worth to you.

Here's what to ask about any exchange, Rankbox's included:

- **No direct swaps.** Two sites trading links with each other is the pattern Google names.
- **Topic match and real articles.** Links should sit inside articles on related sites, not in footers or pages built for links.
- **Verification before credits move,** so you pay only for links that go live.
- **Caps per site.** RankYak, for example, allows one link a month from the same site.
- **Links placed, not credits.** Ask how many links a month a site like yours actually gets.

Rankbox's exchange is built around the first three: no direct swaps, topic-matched placements inside articles, and credits that move only once a link is verified live. A placed link costs 1 to 3 credits, set by the host site's tier. Treat exchange links as a bonus on top of useful content and real [backlinks](/glossary/backlinks) from partners and press.

2. **You manage many sites or clients.** Outrank gives agencies one dashboard for client sites, discounts of up to 20% across sites, a REST API and CLI, and unlimited users in your organization.
3. **You need 60 or 90 articles on one site, or many languages.** Its add-ons cost less per article than SEObot at that volume, and it writes in 150+ languages.
4. **You want human editors, or native publishing to an unusual CMS.** Its Human Curated Service is $1,399 a month, and its [integrations](https://www.outrank.so/integrations) include Notion, Ghost, Framer, WordPress.com, and a Next.js starter.

Outrank also refreshes its own work. Some 2026 lists say it only writes new posts, but its [Article Improvements](https://www.outrank.so/docs/improvements) feature rewrites slipping articles from Search Console data, up to two a week, without using article credits. If one of these is decisive, Outrank's $99 is money well spent. Our [Rankbox vs Outrank page](/alternatives/outrank) covers the rest.

## How to Switch From Outrank

Moving to one of these Outrank alternatives is low-risk if you do it in order. Your published articles live on your own site, so they stay put.

1. **Pull your Search Console data first.** Note which Outrank articles earn impressions and clicks. Those are the pages to protect.
2. **Keep every URL that ranks.** If one must change, set up a 301 redirect. Our [redirect generator](/tools/redirect-generator) writes the rules.
3. **Check your links on both sides.** Ask what happens to links you host for others, links placed for you, and any unspent paid credits once you cancel.
4. **Give old posts an owner.** Article Improvements stops when you leave, so decide who updates slipping posts before [content decay](/glossary/content-decay) sets in.
5. **Mind the billing dates.** Outrank's refund window covers only the 7 days after your first payment. On the $999 yearly plan, you've paid for the year, so plan the switch for renewal.
6. **Run the new tool's trial before Outrank renews.** Read the first few articles closely.
7. **Point the new tool at topics Outrank hasn't covered.** Two articles on the same query compete with each other, a problem called [keyword cannibalization](/glossary/keyword-cannibalization).
