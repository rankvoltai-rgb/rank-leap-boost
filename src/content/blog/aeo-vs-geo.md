Answer engine optimization (AEO) is the work of getting your page lifted out as the direct answer to a question: a featured snippet, a spoken reply, the one-line answer an AI gives. Generative engine optimization (GEO) is the work of getting AI models to include your brand when they write a longer answer from many sources, such as a list of the best tools for a job. AEO wins a passage. GEO wins a place in a shortlist.

The two overlap far more than they differ. Both need pages that search engines can crawl, index and trust. Google goes further: its [guide to generative AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), added on 15 May 2026, says AEO and GEO "are both terms you may see used" for AI search work, and that "optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO." Microsoft splits them differently. Its [advertising guide to AEO and GEO](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/content/dam/sites/msa-about/global/common/content-lib/pdf/from-discovery-to-influence-a-guide-to-aeo-and-geo.pdf) says AEO "is focused on driving clarity" while GEO "helps establish credibility through authoritative voice."

This guide uses the split the two names started with, because it describes two real jobs: extraction and synthesis. It covers a side-by-side table, the shared work, the real differences, how to measure each, a combined plan and a worked example of one page tuned for both. If you only need the tactic list, our [AEO vs GEO tactic sorter](/blog/aeo-vs-geo-differences) places 24 tactics in three columns. For where the labels came from, read [GEO vs AEO: the history of the terms](/blog/geo-vs-aeo).

## Key Takeaways

- Answer engine optimization aims to make one passage the answer. Generative engine optimization aims to get your brand named when an AI blends several sources into one reply.
- AEO grew out of featured snippets, which Google introduced in January 2014, and voice answers. GEO comes from a research paper first posted on 16 November 2023.
- The engine extracts for AEO and synthesises for GEO. That changes the unit that wins: a passage you write versus a reputation built across many sites.
- The base layer is shared: crawling, indexing, clear structure, fresh facts and content worth quoting. Google and Bing both say SEO basics decide eligibility for their AI features.
- GEO leans on signals off your site. Ahrefs found YouTube mentions correlated with AI visibility at about 0.737 across 75,000 brands, and "best X" lists made up 43.8% of the pages ChatGPT used for recommendation prompts.
- Measure AEO per question (do you own the answer?) and GEO per run (how often are you named across many repeats?). AI brand lists repeat so rarely that one check proves little.
- One page can serve both: a tight answer layer for extraction, plus a proof layer of comparisons, sources and corroboration for synthesis.

## AEO and GEO in One Sentence Each

[Answer engine optimization](/glossary/answer-engine-optimization) shapes a page so a system that returns one answer can lift a passage and use it as is. [Generative engine optimization](/glossary/generative-engine-optimization) shapes how AI systems describe and recommend you when they compose an answer from many pages.

### Where answer engine optimization comes from

Google calls a [featured snippet](/glossary/featured-snippet) a result where "the format of a regular search result is reversed, showing the descriptive snippet first." In a [2018 post](https://blog.google/products/search/reintroduction-googles-featured-snippets/), Google said it introduced them in January 2014 and that, for spoken answers, it would "cite the source page in the spoken result." That is the original answer engine: a system that hands over one answer, often out loud.

The label answer engine optimization was in print by 7 February 2018, when [Search Engine Watch](https://www.searchenginewatch.com/2018/02/07/the-rise-of-answer-engine-optimization-why-voice-search-matters/) wrote that voice search was turning search engines into "answer engines." Our post on [where GEO and AEO came from](/blog/geo-vs-aeo) traces the term further back.

### Where generative engine optimization comes from

GEO was named in ["GEO: Generative Engine Optimization"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) by Pranjal Aggarwal and five co-authors, first posted on 16 November 2023 and accepted at KDD 2024. The paper describes engines that answer "by synthesizing information from multiple sources and summarizing them using LLMs." Its authors reported that changes such as adding citations, quotations and statistics could lift a source's visibility in those answers "by up to 40%."

### Why vendors now use the names loosely

In 2026 many vendors use answer engine optimization for all AI answer work. HubSpot's [AEO product](https://www.hubspot.com/products/marketing/aeo-guide) defines it as improving "how often and how accurately your business appears in AI-generated answers on AI engines like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity." Profound [argues the two are "the same thing"](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/aeo-vs-geo). Neither view is wrong. This guide keeps the older split because it tells you what to do differently, not just what to call it.

## AEO vs. GEO Side by Side

The table is the short version of this whole guide. Read across a row to see how one aspect changes between the two jobs.

|                                  | Answer engine optimization (AEO)                                                                | Generative engine optimization (GEO)                                                        |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Goal                             | Your passage becomes the answer                                                                 | Your brand is named, cited and described correctly in a composed answer                     |
| Typical question                 | "How do you invoice a retainer?" One right answer                                               | "Best invoicing tool for agencies?" Several valid options                                   |
| Main surfaces                    | Featured snippets, People Also Ask, spoken answers, one-line AI replies                         | ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, Claude, AI Overviews and AI Mode                      |
| How the engine uses your content | Extracts a passage close to word for word                                                       | Synthesises: blends facts from many pages and rewrites them                                 |
| Unit that wins                   | A passage on your page                                                                          | Your brand's standing across your site and other sites                                      |
| Signals that matter most         | Clear question and answer, format that fits the query, being indexed and eligible for a snippet | Mentions on cited sites, list placements, reviews, consistent facts, evidence in your pages |
| What you control                 | Almost all of it: it lives on your page                                                         | Part of it: much of it lives on other sites                                                 |
| How it is measured               | Per question: do you own the answer?                                                            | Per run: how often are you named across many repeats?                                       |
| Typical tactics                  | Question headings, answer-first sentences, lists and tables, fact pages                         | Comparison pages, cited statistics, digital PR, roundup placements, entity cleanup          |
| Common failure                   | A good answer buried under an intro                                                             | A strong page that no other site mentions                                                   |

Two rows deserve a closer look: how the engine uses your content, and the unit that wins. Everything else follows from them.

## Extraction vs. Synthesis: How Each Engine Uses Your Content

### Extraction rewards the cleanest passage

An extracting system picks one passage and shows it with little change. Google's [featured snippet docs](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/featured-snippets) say "Google does not provide an exact minimum length required," so there is no magic word count. What helps is a heading that matches the question and a first sentence that answers it on its own.

The same habit shows up in AI answers. In Kevin Indig's analysis of 18,012 verified ChatGPT citations, [reported by Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/chatgpt-citations-content-study-469483) in February 2026, 44.2% of citations came from the first 30% of a page, and cited passages were nearly twice as likely to use clear definitions such as "X is." That is answer engine optimization at work inside a generative engine.

### Synthesis rewards being one of the sources

A synthesising system reads several pages and writes something new. For a multi-option question it builds a shortlist, and the shortlist moves. In a [January 2026 SparkToro study](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/), 600 volunteers ran 12 prompts 2,961 times across ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI. There was less than a 1-in-100 chance that ChatGPT or Google's AI would return the same list of brands twice.

Your page alone rarely decides that list. Google's Robby Stein put it plainly in an [October 2025 interview](https://searchengineland.com/seo-ai-search-optimization-overlap-robby-stein-464028): "If you're a business and you're mentioned in top business lists or from a public article that lots of people end up finding, those kinds of things become useful for the AI to find."

### Why the line keeps blurring

The surfaces that defined AEO are turning generative. Apple released [Siri AI in beta on 14 September 2026](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/siri-ai-a-profoundly-more-capable-and-personal-assistant-is-here/), and Apple says it "can also use broad world knowledge to get up-to-date information on virtually any topic." Google's AI Overviews can quote a passage and build a list in the same reply. Our [voice search checklist for 2026](/blog/voice-search-optimization-2026) covers what that means for spoken answers. The practical result: most pages now need both jobs done at once.

## Where AEO and GEO Overlap (Most of the Work)

Start here, because this layer decides whether answer engine optimization or GEO is possible at all. Google says a page must be "indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet" to appear in its AI features. Bing's [webmaster guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a) say the same SEO practices "also support eligibility for AI-generated experiences, grounding results, and citations."

The shared work falls into five groups:

1. **Access.** Search crawlers can fetch the page. OpenAI says sites that block OAI-SearchBot ["will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers"](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) except as navigational links.
2. **Indexing.** The page is in Google's and Bing's indexes, with sitemaps and accurate update dates.
3. **Readable structure.** Headings, short paragraphs, real lists and tables, and main text in the HTML rather than loaded by scripts.
4. **Content worth quoting.** Google's guide says unique, useful content "will likely influence your website's presence in generative AI search in the long run more than any of the other suggestions in this guide."
5. **Current facts.** Prices, specs and policies that match what you say everywhere else.

None of this is new SEO, and that's the point. Our post on [answer engine optimization vs SEO](/blog/answer-engine-optimization-vs-seo) lists what carries over from classic search work and what changes. In our [tactic sorter](/blog/aeo-vs-geo-differences), 12 of the 24 tactics help both jobs, and the list of things to skip is the same for each: keyword stuffing, invented mentions and AI-only files for Google. Google lists llms.txt files, content "chunking" and inauthentic mentions as things you can ignore for its search.

## Where AEO and GEO Differ

### The unit that wins

Answer engine optimization is won or lost inside one section of one page. You can fix it in an afternoon. GEO is won across many pages, most of which you don't own. You can't edit a roundup on someone else's site, but you can earn a place in it.

### The signals that decide it

For extraction, the deciding signals are on the page: does a passage answer the question cleanly, in the format the question wants?

For synthesis, signals off the page carry more weight. Ahrefs' [December 2025 study of 75,000 brands](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations/) found YouTube mentions correlated with visibility in ChatGPT, AI Mode and AI Overviews at about 0.737, the strongest factor it tested, while link and content volume had little impact. Its [study of 750 recommendation prompts](https://ahrefs.com/blog/best-lists-research/) found "best X" blog lists made up 43.8% of the page types ChatGPT used. These are correlations and samples, not proof of cause, but they point the GEO half of the work off your site. Our guide to [co-citation in AI answers](/blog/link-building-ai-visibility-co-citation) grades that evidence, and our [co-citation SEO playbook](/blog/co-citation-seo) shows how to earn places on those lists.

### The question shape

AEO questions have one right answer: a definition, a step, a number, a policy. GEO questions have many valid answers: "best", "alternatives to", "X vs Y", "which tool for". If a buyer could reasonably accept three different brands, you are in GEO territory.

### What a win looks like

An AEO win is visible and stable enough to screenshot: your sentence in the snippet box. A GEO win is a rate. You are named in, say, 30% of runs for a prompt, and that rate moves slowly as the web around you changes.

## How to Measure Answer Engine Optimization and GEO

The two jobs need different counting, because one result is fairly fixed and the other varies on every run.

|                     | AEO                                                                                                                                                                                                    | GEO                                                                                         |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Core question       | Is our passage the answer?                                                                                                                                                                             | Are we in the shortlist, and described correctly?                                           |
| What you count      | Questions: owned or not owned                                                                                                                                                                          | Answers: named or not, across repeated runs                                                 |
| Headline metric     | Answer ownership rate: tracked questions where your page supplies the snippet or quoted answer, divided by all tracked questions                                                                       | Share of Model: answers that name you, divided by all answers to unbranded category prompts |
| Supporting metrics  | People Also Ask appearances; which section an AI quoted                                                                                                                                                | Citation rate, average position, accuracy rate                                              |
| Where to read it    | Rank tracker SERP-feature reports, Search Console                                                                                                                                                      | A prompt panel run by hand or with a tracker                                                |
| Google's own report | Search Console's [generative AI performance report](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports), launched 3 June 2026, shows impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode | Same report for Google; other engines give site owners no citation report                   |

Answer ownership rate is a plain ratio this guide uses, not an industry standard. For the GEO side, our [GEO Metrics Framework](/blog/geo-metrics-framework) defines Share of Model and eleven other metrics with formulas, and our guide to [measuring GEO](/blog/how-to-measure-geo) covers panel size, run counts and a control test that separates your work from engine drift.

One rule covers both: never report a single check. A featured snippet can change hands next week, and an AI shortlist can change on the next run.

## A Combined Plan for Both

This is Rankbox's five-step plan for doing answer engine optimization and GEO as one program, not two. Each step builds on the one before.

1. **Week 1: Fix the shared base.** Confirm crawler access, indexing in Google and Bing, and that key text is in the raw HTML. Our [AEO audit](/blog/aeo-audit) has a 20-point check for this.
2. **Week 2: Sort your questions.** List 30 to 50 buyer questions. Mark each one "single answer" (AEO) or "several options" (GEO). The free [AI Question Generator](/tools/ai-question-generator) and [AI Visibility Prompt Kit](/tools/ai-visibility-prompt-generator) help you start the list.
3. **Weeks 3 to 6: Build the answer layer.** For every single-answer question, give it a question heading and a first sentence that answers it with a fact. Our guide to [optimizing content for AI search](/blog/optimize-content-for-ai-search) shows the passage edits.
4. **Weeks 3 to 12: Build the proof layer.** For every multi-option question, publish a fair comparison or "how to choose" page, add cited evidence, and pitch the roundups, review sites and creators that engines already cite for those prompts. Our guide to [ranking in ChatGPT's recommendation lists](/blog/how-to-rank-on-chatgpt) goes deep on this half.
5. **Every month: Measure both ways.** Track answer ownership for the AEO questions and Share of Model for the GEO prompts. Compare the prompts you worked on with ones you left alone.

The order matters. Steps 1 and 3 are the answer engine optimization half: fast and fully in your control. Step 4 is slow and partly outside it, so it should start early and run longer.

## Worked Example: One Tallyfold Page Tuned for Both

Tallyfold is a fictional invoicing and payments app for agencies. Its rivals, Brindlework and Kestrelyn, are fictional too. Tallyfold costs $39 a month with 3 users included, then $12 for each extra user. All results below are illustrative.

### The page and its two jobs

Tallyfold has one page on retainer invoicing. It should win two kinds of question:

- **Single-answer questions (AEO):** 12 tracked questions, such as "what should a retainer invoice include?" and "how much does Tallyfold cost for a five-person agency?"
- **Multi-option prompts (GEO):** 20 unbranded prompts, such as "best invoicing software for agencies on retainers," run in three engines, twice each: 120 answers per reading.

At baseline, the page owns the answer for 2 of 12 questions (17%). Tallyfold is named in 14 of 120 answers (11.7%).

### The Dual-Fit Page Scorecard

This is Rankbox's 10-point check for a page that must be both extracted and included. Five checks serve the answer layer, five serve the proof layer. Score 1 for a pass.

| Layer  | Check                                                                  | Before      | After       |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ----------- |
| Answer | Each target question has its own question-shaped heading               | 0           | 1           |
| Answer | The first sentence under it answers alone, naming the subject          | 0           | 1           |
| Answer | Each answer carries a specific fact: a number, date or step count      | 1           | 1           |
| Answer | The format fits the question: steps as a list, specs as a table        | 0           | 1           |
| Answer | No snippet controls (`nosnippet`, low `max-snippet`) block the passage | 0           | 1           |
| Proof  | A fair comparison with named rivals, dated, with their strengths       | 0           | 1           |
| Proof  | Claims cite sources, with at least one statistic or quote              | 0           | 1           |
| Proof  | Company facts match the site's other pages and public profiles         | 1           | 1           |
| Proof  | Two or more independent sites mention the brand for this topic         | 0           | 0           |
| Proof  | A visible "last updated" date on the facts that change                 | 0           | 1           |
|        | **Total**                                                              | **2 of 10** | **9 of 10** |

### What Tallyfold changed

- **Answer layer.** The 180-word story that opened the page moved below the answers. The pricing question now has its own heading and a one-line answer: "A five-person agency pays $63 a month: $39 for the first three users plus 2 × $12." The retainer steps became a five-item numbered list.
- **Proof layer.** A comparison table now sets Tallyfold beside Brindlework and Kestrelyn, with each one's best use and a "checked on" date. Tallyfold added a figure from its own customer data on how fast retainer invoices get paid, with how it was measured.
- **Off the page.** Tallyfold pitched three "best agency invoicing" roundups and recorded a product walkthrough for YouTube. The ninth check still fails: after eight weeks, only one roundup has added it.

### The result, eight weeks later

The page now owns the answer for 6 of 12 questions (50%). Tallyfold is named in 31 of 120 answers (25.8%), up 14.1 points.

Is the GEO change real? With two samples of 120 answers, the margin of error on the difference is about ±9.7 points at 95% confidence, so a 14.1-point rise clears it. The AEO change is easier to read, but 12 questions is a small set, so Tallyfold checks which questions moved rather than leaning on the percentage. The last failing check points to the next job: more independent mentions, which is GEO work no page edit can finish.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between AEO and GEO?

AEO makes a single passage the answer to a question with one right answer, as in a featured snippet or spoken reply. GEO gets your brand included when an AI writes a longer answer from many sources, such as a shortlist of tools. AEO is mostly on-page; GEO depends heavily on what other sites say about you.

### Is answer engine optimization the same as GEO?

Not in origin, but often in practice. Answer engine optimization grew from featured snippets and voice answers; GEO comes from a 2023 research paper on AI-written answers. Many vendors now use either name for all AI search work, and Google calls both "still SEO." Most tactics serve both.

### Which should I do first, AEO or GEO?

Do the shared base first: crawler access, indexing and clear pages. Then do answer engine optimization on your top pages, because it's fast and fully in your control. Start GEO's off-site work in parallel, since mentions and roundup placements take months to build.

### Does answer engine optimization still matter for voice search?

Yes, though voice is changing. Google said in 2018 that spoken featured snippets cite the source page, and assistants such as Siri AI now draw on the web to answer. Short, self-contained answers still suit being read aloud. Google's speakable markup remains a beta for US English news on Google Home devices.

### How do you measure AEO and GEO?

Measure AEO per question: the share of tracked questions where your page supplies the snippet or quoted answer. Measure GEO per run: the share of answers to unbranded prompts that name you, across repeated runs in each engine. Google's Search Console also reports AI Overviews and AI Mode impressions.

### Do I need different content for AEO and GEO?

Usually not. One page can carry both layers: clear question-and-answer sections for extraction, plus comparisons, cited evidence and current facts for synthesis. What GEO adds is work off the page, such as earning mentions in the lists and reviews AI engines read.
