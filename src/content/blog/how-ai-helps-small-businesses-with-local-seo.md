AI helps small businesses with local SEO by taking on the slow writing and checking jobs: review replies, profile posts, page drafts, listing audits, schema, alt text, keyword ideas and checks on what AI assistants say about you. It doesn't replace your judgment. Most uses below come with a rule set by Google, Yelp or the FTC that decides where the help stops.

The upside is real for a one- or two-person business. Most local SEO work is small, repeated writing that gets skipped when the phones are busy. An AI chatbot can draft a review reply in seconds, and you can fix it in a minute. The risk is also real: fake reviews, copy-paste city pages and invented facts can cost you your listing.

This guide gives eight uses, each with a prompt or free tool, a time estimate and the guardrail. It ends with a monthly routine for a made-up dog groomer. For how AI assistants like ChatGPT choose which nearby businesses to recommend, see our full guide to [local SEO in ChatGPT and AI search](/blog/local-seo-in-chatgpt).

## Key Takeaways

- Use AI to draft, compare and describe local SEO work. Keep a person in charge of every fact, price and promise that gets published.
- Never use AI to write reviews. The FTC's 2024 rule bans fake reviews, naming AI-generated ones, and Google removes fake engagement.
- AI-drafted city pages are fine when each one holds real, local facts. Near-identical pages for each town match Google's definition of doorway abuse.
- Google Business Profile posts are a good AI job. Q&A isn't: Google shut down its Q&A API on 3 November 2025, and the Q&A help page is gone.
- A monthly AI routine for a small shop takes about 3 hours 23 minutes, by our estimate, with checks built in.
- Check what ChatGPT, Gemini and other assistants say about you once a month. It's the one use where AI helps you watch AI.

## Reviews, Posts and Local SEO on Your Profile

### 1. Draft review replies

Replies are the easiest job to fall behind on. Google's [review tips](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122) ask for short, personal, "conversational, not promotional" replies, and warn against sending "the same 'thank you' to everyone." AI is good at exactly that kind of variety.

> Draft a reply to this Google review for [business]. Under 60 words. Thank them by first name, mention the specific thing they praised, and don't offer a discount. Review: "[paste]"

For a negative review, add your side of the facts and ask for a calm reply that invites them to call. Read every draft before posting. Google [checks replies against its content policies](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474050) before they go live, which can take up to 30 days in rare cases.

**Guardrail:** AI writes replies, never reviews. The FTC's [final rule on fake reviews](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) covers reviews by people who don't exist, "such as AI-generated fake reviews." Google's [Maps content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) bans fake engagement, rewards for reviews, and asking customers to include "specific content." Don't use AI to script what customers should say.

### 2. Write Google Business Profile posts

Business Profile [posts](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7342169) come in three types: Update, Offer and Event. You can schedule them and set them to repeat weekly or monthly. That makes them a natural batch job: one session with AI can draft a month of posts.

> Write four Google Business Profile updates for [business] in [town] for [month]. One seasonal tip, one new service, one staff or behind-the-scenes note, one reminder about [booking or hours]. 80 words each, plain language, no hashtags.

**Guardrail:** Google's own tip is to avoid "text that adds no value, and auto-generated or distracting content." So give the AI real material: a new service, a seasonal fact, a date. Posts are reviewed before they show.

A note on Q&A. Many local SEO guides still say to seed your profile's questions and answers. Google [discontinued its Q&A API](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/qanda/change-log) on 3 November 2025, "in the process of updating the Q&A functionality," and its old Q&A help page now returns "this page can't be found." Put your common questions and answers on your own website instead, where you control them.

## Local SEO Work on Your Website

### 3. Draft local landing pages

A page for each town you serve can help people find you. It can also get you in trouble. Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) list "multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as doorway abuse. Using AI "to generate many pages without adding value for users" is scaled content abuse.

The fix is to feed the AI facts it can't invent. Before you prompt, write down for that town: jobs you've done there, travel fee, typical response time, local rules or permits, and one customer story you have permission to share.

> Using only the notes below, outline a page for [service] in [town]. Lead with a two-sentence answer to "do you serve [town] and how fast can you get there." Flag any claim that isn't in my notes. Notes: [paste]

**Guardrail:** If two town pages would read the same with the name swapped, merge them. Google's [guidance on AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content) says to "focus on accuracy, quality, and relevance, especially when automatically generating the content."

### 4. Generate LocalBusiness schema

Schema markup is a block of code that states your name, address, hours and coordinates in a form machines read. AI can turn your fact sheet into it in seconds.

> Turn these business facts into LocalBusiness JSON-LD. Use the most specific schema.org type for [trade]. Include geo coordinates with five decimal places, opening hours and phone with country code. Don't add ratings or reviews. Facts: [paste]

**Guardrail:** Check the output against Google's [LocalBusiness rules](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business): `name` and `address` are required, coordinates need at least five decimal places, and ratings markup is only for sites that review other businesses. If the output rates your own business with `aggregateRating`, delete it. Then test the page in Google's Rich Results Test. Our free [schema generator](/tools/schema-generator) builds the block from a form if you'd rather skip the prompt. Our [guide to local SEO in ChatGPT](/blog/local-seo-in-chatgpt) explains why this markup is insurance rather than a ranking lever for AI answers.

### 5. Write photo alt text

Alt text describes an image for people using screen readers and for search engines. Google calls it [the most important attribute](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-images) for image metadata. It's easy to leave blank when a site has dozens of photos.

Upload a batch of photos to a chatbot that reads images:

> Write alt text for each photo, under 125 characters, describing what's actually shown. Mention [town] only if the photo shows a local landmark.

**Guardrail:** Google warns against stuffing alt attributes with keywords. Read each line. AI sometimes guesses a breed, a product or a place that isn't there.

## Local SEO Research and Audits

### 6. Audit your listings for matching facts

NAP stands for name, address and phone. When they differ across listings, AI assistants and maps may state the wrong one. Copy the text from your Google, Apple Business, Bing Places, Yelp and Facebook pages into one document, then ask:

> Compare these five listings with my fact sheet. Make a table of every field that differs: name, address, phone, hours, website, category. Don't correct anything; just list the differences.

That table becomes your to-do list. For the full fact sheet and how to score each fix, see our guide to [optimizing your business for AI search engines](/blog/optimize-business-for-ai-search).

**Guardrail:** AI reads what you paste, so paste the live text, not your memory of it. You make every edit yourself, in each platform's dashboard. Bing Places can import from Google, which saves a step if your Google profile is right.

### 7. Brainstorm local keywords

AI is good at listing the ways people describe a service: "mobile dog grooming," "dog groomer that takes big breeds," "puppy's first groom near me." Then check which ones real people use.

> List 40 ways customers in [town] might search for [service], including questions, problems and neighborhood names. Group them by intent: urgent, price, quality, specific need.

Check the list against your Google Search Console Performance report, which shows [the queries that bring visitors to your site](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553). Google's Keyword Planner shows search estimates too, but it sits inside Google Ads and [asks for billing details](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243) before you can use it.

**Guardrail:** If a chatbot gives you search volumes, treat them as guesses. It has no access to real counts unless a tool feeds them in.

### 8. Check what AI assistants say about your business

This is the newest local SEO job, and the one where AI checks on AI. In BrightLocal's [March 2026 report](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/lcrs-ai-trust/), 45% of consumers said they had used AI tools for local business recommendations, up from 6% a year earlier.

Once a month, run a few prompts in ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot, plus Siri if your customers use iPhones: "[service] near me," "best [service] in [town]," and "what time does [business] close on Saturday." Note whether you're named and whether the facts are right. Run each prompt more than once, because answers change. Our free [AI Visibility Prompt Kit](/tools/ai-visibility-prompt-generator) builds a starter set with a scorecard.

When a fact is wrong, the fix is usually in a listing, not on your site. Our local SEO guide for AI assistants has a table of which listing feeds which assistant. For ChatGPT alone, see [how to get cited by ChatGPT as a local business](/blog/how-to-get-cited-by-chatgpt-as-a-local-business).

**Guardrail:** One answer is one sample. Judge trends across several runs and several weeks.

## Example: Tansywick Grooming's Monthly AI Routine

Tansywick Grooming is a made-up, two-groomer dog salon in Asheville, North Carolina (tansywickgrooming.example). It gets about 24 Google reviews a month, posts weekly, serves three nearby towns and adds around 20 photos a month. Here is its local SEO routine, using the time estimates above.

| Task                                    | Volume per month | Minutes each | Minutes per month |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------ | ----------------- |
| Review replies                          | 24               | 2            | 48                |
| Business Profile posts                  | 4                | 10           | 40                |
| One town page per quarter (2 hours ÷ 3) | 1/3              | 120          | 40                |
| Listing audit per quarter (30 ÷ 3)      | 1/3              | 30           | 10                |
| Photo alt text                          | 20               | 1            | 20                |
| Keyword review per quarter (45 ÷ 3)     | 1/3              | 45           | 15                |
| AI answer check                         | 1                | 30           | 30                |
| **Total**                               |                  |              | **203**           |

That's 203 minutes, or about 3 hours 23 minutes a month. The schema block is a one-off 15 minutes, so it isn't in the monthly total. The biggest line is review replies, which is also the line customers see most.

Two rules keep the routine honest. The owner reads every reply before it posts. And no town page goes live unless it holds at least three facts only Tansywick could know, such as its travel fee to that town.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use ChatGPT to write Google reviews for my business?

No. Reviews must come from real customers describing a real experience. The FTC's rule bans fake reviews, naming AI-generated ones, and Google removes fake engagement and reviews from people with a conflict of interest, such as staff. Use AI to reply to reviews, never to write them.

### Is AI-generated content bad for local SEO?

Not by itself. Google's spam policy targets unhelpful pages made at scale "no matter how it's created," so the test is value, not the tool. It becomes a problem when AI churns out many near-identical pages, such as one thin page per town, which Google's spam policies call doorway or scaled content abuse. Add real local facts to every page.

### Should I let AI reply to my reviews automatically?

It's safer to approve each reply yourself. AI can misread sarcasm, promise things you don't offer or repeat private details. Google reviews each reply before posting, but that check is for policy, not for accuracy. A two-minute read per reply is cheap insurance.

### Can AI replace a local SEO agency?

It can replace some of the writing and checking time, not the decisions. AI won't claim your listings, earn reviews, fix a suspended profile or know that your van no longer serves a town. Many small businesses use AI for routine tasks and pay for expert help on problems.

### Does Google penalize AI-written Business Profile posts?

Google doesn't ban AI help for posts, but its posts guidance warns against "auto-generated or distracting content," and every post is reviewed before it shows. Posts built on real news, offers and dates pass. Generic filler adds nothing for customers and may not be approved.

### What's the easiest local SEO task to start with AI?

Review replies. You already have the reviews, the replies are short, and the benefit shows right away on your profile. Start there, then add monthly posts once you have a prompt that sounds like you.
