## Why People Look for Frase Alternatives

Frase does more in 2026 than it ever has. When people compare Frase alternatives, their reasons are mostly about fit and price. These come up most.

### Old reviews describe a different Frase

Frase relaunched on 2 February 2026 as "the New Frase," a ground-up rebuild with an AI agent, CMS publishing, and AI visibility tracking. Several pages that rank for "Frase alternatives" still describe the old app. One quotes a $14.99 starting price and says Frase doesn't watch AI answers. Another says Frase outputs a document and leaves publishing to other tools.

Neither matches Frase today. Its [pricing page](https://www.frase.io/pricing) starts at $49 a month, tracks AI answers on every plan, and publishes to WordPress, Webflow, Sanity, and Wix. If a list of Frase alternatives gets those basics wrong, check its other facts too.

### Ten articles on the entry plan

Starter costs $49 a month, or $39 billed yearly, for 10 articles, one site, and one seat. That's $4.90 an article on monthly billing. Thirty articles a month means Professional, at $129 for 40.

Starter holds at its limits, so there are no surprise bills, but there's also no way to buy a few extra articles. Pay-as-you-go overage is only offered on Professional, at $5 an article, and on Scale, at $4. Internal linking suggestions and the content calendar also start at Professional.

### AI tracking depends on the plan

Frase checks your tracked prompts daily. Starter covers ChatGPT and Google AI, with 50 prompts. Perplexity joins on Professional, with 200. Claude and Gemini arrive only on Scale, at $299 a month for 500 prompts.

That's generous at the low end. But all five engines sit on the top self-serve plan. [Prompt tracking](/glossary/prompt-tracking) is only as useful as the engines your buyers actually use, so check which ones you need before you pick a tier.

### The legacy app and lifetime deals

The rebuilt Frase runs at a new address. The legacy app stays at app.frase.io, and Frase's [migration guide](https://docs.frase.io/legacy-migration-guide) says there's "no required deadline" to move, though some older plans are "ineligible for migration." On [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.frase.io), Frase had 3.7 out of 5 from 63 reviews when checked on 21 September 2026. Some low reviews come from lifetime-deal holders who say they were left on the legacy app, while other recent reviews praise the AI tracker.

### Approvals come first

Frase's pricing page says its agent drafts and auto-publishes on every plan, and you switch auto-publishing on per content type. But the product is built around a person approving work. Its [automation docs](https://docs.frase.io/automations/automations) describe earned autonomy: after "5 approvals at 90%+ in 30 days," Frase suggests "Publish without review," and it pauses itself when drafts get rejected.

That's a careful design, and a good one for teams. It's more setup than you want if you'd rather not open a content tool at all. That's the gap the one autopilot among these Frase alternatives fills.

Sources are cited inline and in a References list. Every draft is re-run against its failing SEO checks until it passes the [SEO and GEO score](/features/seo-geo-score), and each one opens with a direct answer and ends with an FAQ.

- **Price:** $49.50 a month for 30 articles on one site, with a 7-day free trial that takes a card. Extra sites cost $49.50 a month each.
- **Cadence:** you set a weekly schedule, up to one article a day.

**Trade-offs:** read this part closely if you're coming from Frase. Frase tracks your prompts in AI answers today, from its $49 plan. Rankbox doesn't monitor AI citations yet; it grades every article for AI-answer readiness instead. Frase's editor, with live SEO, GEO, and EEAT scores, suits teams who write, and coaching writers through a draft is not Rankbox's focus. Rankbox also writes new articles rather than refreshing old ones, has one login per account, and estimates keyword demand with AI rather than a keyword database.

**Trade-offs:** publishing is not its focus. Articles export to WordPress as drafts by default, and automatic publishing needs Zapier or the API on Peace of Mind. Claude isn't one of its tracked engines.

**Best for:** content teams of three to ten who work each draft by hand and want keyword data in the same tool. The right pick among Frase alternatives depends on which job you were using Frase for. Match the job to the tool.

- **Nobody on your team writes, and you want articles delivered:** Rankbox, at $49.50 a month for 30.
- **Your team writes and plans by keyword data:** Surfer SEO, on Standard or Pro.
- **You have many writers and want one simple grade:** Clearscope.
- **You want an editor on a tight budget:** NeuronWriter, from $23 a month.
- **You're tuning pages that already exist:** PageOptimizer Pro.
- **You already pay for Semrush:** the Content Toolkit, at a flat $60.
- **You want tracking and articles in one bill for less than Frase Professional:** Scalenut Plus, at $89.

- **You plan content across a large enterprise site:** MarketMuse, by demo.

What helps most is a direct answer, facts with sources, and the questions buyers ask. ## When Frase Is Still the Right Choice

Frase does several things better than most of the Frase alternatives here. It's worth staying if these describe you.

1. **You need AI citation tracking and content in one bill.** Frase tracks 50 prompts daily in ChatGPT and Google AI from $49, with cited URLs and their positions. Rankbox doesn't monitor citations yet.
2. **Your team writes and edits.** Frase's editor scores SEO, GEO, and [E-E-A-T](/glossary/e-e-a-t) from 0 to 100, and writers can approve facts and sources before the agent drafts. If people on your team write, Frase supports them better than an autopilot does.
3. **You're protecting a large library.** Frase audits 50 to 1,000 pages a month by plan. Its Content Guard spots ranking drops in Search Console data and drafts the fix for your approval, so [content decay](/glossary/content-decay) is handled in the same tool.
4. **You run several sites, in several languages, or from code.** Professional covers 5 sites and 3 seats, and Frase writes in 70+ languages. Every plan includes an API, an MCP server, and a CLI, and it publishes natively to WordPress, Webflow, Sanity, and Wix.

If one of those is decisive, Frase is money well spent. Our [Rankbox vs Frase page](/alternatives/frase) breaks the differences down further.

## How to Switch From Frase

Moving to one of these Frase alternatives is low-risk if you do it in order. Pages you've published to your own CMS stay where they are.

1. **Pull your Search Console data first.** Note which pages get impressions and clicks. Those are the pages to protect.
2. **Export your briefs and drafts.** Frase exports to Google Docs, Notion, HTML, Markdown, and PDF. Do it while your plan is active.
3. **Check for pages hosted on FraseCMS.** If Frase hosts your blog, moving means new hosting and a 301 redirect for every URL that changes. Our [redirect generator](/tools/redirect-generator) writes the rules.
4. **Save your AI visibility baseline.** Frase keeps 3 months of history on Professional and 12 on Scale, and current data only on Starter. Record your prompt results so you can compare later.
5. **Time the cancellation.** Frase doesn't refund, and downgrades apply at the next cycle, so cancel just before renewal. If you're still on the legacy app, there's no deadline to move.
6. **Run the new tool's trial before Frase renews.** Read the first few articles or reports closely.
7. **Point the new tool at topics you haven't covered.** Two pages targeting the same query compete with each other, a problem called [keyword cannibalization](/glossary/keyword-cannibalization).
