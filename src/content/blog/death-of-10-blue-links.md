The 10 blue links aren't dead. What died is their role as the finished product of a search. Between 1998 and 2026, search engines moved through three jobs: indexing the web so you could find a URL, ranking pages so you could pick the best one, and synthesizing an answer so you might not need to click at all. In 2025 and 2026, a new job appeared at the edge of the third: agents that search, read and act for you.

The links are still there, one layer down. Google says its AI features are ["rooted in our core Search ranking and quality systems"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide). When AI Mode isn't confident in an answer, Google says the response ["will be a set of web search results"](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/ai-mode-search/) instead. And ChatGPT search ["typically rewrites your query into one or more targeted queries"](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-chatgpt-search) that it sends to search providers. Most AI answers are written on top of ranked lists of links that the reader never sees.

## Key Takeaways

- "10 blue links" describes Google's classic results page: ten ranked links to other sites. Google itself wrote in 2018 that the format "doesn't work as well" for mobile and voice searches.
- Search has had three eras: indexing (1998 to 2006), ranking (2007 to 2021) and synthesis (2022 onward). Each changed what a search hands back: a URL, a ranked page, then a written answer.
- The death of 10 blue links has been announced before. Blended results in 2007, the Knowledge Graph in 2012 and featured snippets in 2014 all pushed links down the page.
- Blue links still carry most results pages. Pew found an AI summary on 18% of Google searches in March 2025, and SparkToro put AI Overviews on "20%+" of searches in early 2026.
- AI answers run on ranked results. Google says its generative features rely on its core ranking systems, and ChatGPT search sends rewritten queries to search providers.
- Fewer searches end in a click. In Similarweb's US panel, 68% of Google searches ended without one from January to April 2026, against about 60% in 2024.
- Our 2027 forecast, labelled as ours: blue links survive as the fallback and proof layer, while agents that search in the background become a visible share of site visits.

## Where "10 Blue Links" Came From, and How Often It Has "Died"

The phrase describes the page Google made famous: a search box, then a stack of ten results, each a blue title linking to someone else's site. The idea behind the order came from a [1998 paper](http://infolab.stanford.edu/~backrub/google.html) by Sergey Brin and Lawrence Page. Their prototype held "a full text and hyperlink database of at least 24 million pages" and used PageRank, which they called "an excellent way to prioritize the results of web keyword searches."

The page never stayed pure for long. Paid links arrived first. Google's [AdWords launch](https://googlepress.blogspot.com/2000/10/google-launches-self-service.html) in October 2000 put self-serve keyword ads on the results page. After that, the "death of 10 blue links" was announced at least three times before anyone had heard of ChatGPT:

1. **2007: blended results.** Google's [Universal Search release](https://googlepress.blogspot.com/2007/05/google-begins-move-to-universal-search_16.html) promised "a single set of blended search results" mixing videos, images, news, maps and books. Its stated goal was to "provide the very best answer every time a user enters a query."
2. **2012: facts on the page.** The [Knowledge Graph](https://blog.google/products/search/introducing-knowledge-graph-things-not/) let people "instantly get information" about 500 million things on the results page itself.
3. **2014: answers above the links.** Featured snippets put an extracted answer above the first result. When Google [reintroduced them in 2018](https://blog.google/products/search/reintroduction-googles-featured-snippets/), it said that on mobile and voice, "the traditional '10 blue links' format doesn't work as well."

So each claim that the 10 blue links are dead is part of a 19-year story. The results page kept adding boxes, and the links kept moving down. What changed after 2022 is that the answer is no longer copied from one page. A model writes it from many pages, and that breaks the old link between ranking first and getting the visit.

## The Three-Era Model: Indexing, Ranking, Synthesis

Most writing on this shift jumps from "Google" to "ChatGPT" and skips the part that explains it. Rankbox's Three-Era Model sorts 28 years of search by the job the engine does for the searcher. Each era changes what you compete for, and what counts as winning.

|                           | Era 1: Indexing                    | Era 2: Ranking                                               | Era 3: Synthesis                                                              |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Years                     | 1998 to 2006                       | 2007 to 2021                                                 | 2022 to today                                                                 |
| The engine's main job     | Find and store pages.              | Order pages by relevance and quality, and add features.      | Read pages and write the answer.                                              |
| What the searcher gets    | The 10 blue links: a list of URLs. | The 10 blue links plus boxes: images, news, facts, snippets. | A written answer with a few sources.                                          |
| Typical need served       | Navigational: reach a site.        | Informational and commercial: find the best page.            | Task completion: get the answer, or the action.                               |
| What you compete for      | Being in the index.                | A position from 1 to 10.                                     | A mention or citation inside the answer.                                      |
| The scoreboard            | Pages indexed.                     | Rank and [click-through rate](/glossary/click-through-rate). | Mentions, citations and [AI referral traffic](/glossary/ai-referral-traffic). |
| Who reads your page first | A crawler, then a person.          | A ranking system, then a person.                             | A model, and maybe a person later.                                            |

The eras overlap. Google still indexes and ranks, and it now synthesizes on top. What moves is the finish line. In Era 1, the search ended when you found the site. In Era 2, it ended when you clicked the best result. In Era 3, it can end without a click, because the answer is on the screen.

A fourth job is forming at the edge of Era 3: agentic retrieval. Here a model plans its own searches, reads the results, searches again and sometimes acts on a site. Microsoft used the term for a product in May 2025, describing a [query engine](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/azure-ai-foundry-blog/introducing-agentic-retrieval-in-azure-ai-search/4414677) that "plans and runs its own retrieval strategy" using the conversation history. We treat it as the frontier of synthesis, not a fourth era yet, because it still reads the same ranked results.

## The Evidence That the 10 Blue Links Still Exist

The title of this post is a provocation, so here is the other side. As of September 2026, the 10 blue links are shrinking in reach but not gone. The evidence comes from four directions.

### They still lead most results pages

Google says AI Overviews appear only when its systems judge them "additive to classic Search," and ["as such, often don't trigger."](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) Independent counts agree. In [Pew Research Center's study](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) of 900 US adults' browsing in March 2025, 18% of Google searches produced an AI summary. SparkToro's [June 2026 analysis](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/) of Similarweb's US panel put AI Overviews on "20%+ of all searches." Most searches still show a classic page first.

AI Mode is growing fast, but from a small base. The same SparkToro analysis found that only 0.34% of Google searches reached AI Mode from January to April 2026, even as Google reported one billion monthly AI Mode users.

### Google kept a way to see only links

On [14 May 2024](https://9to5google.com/2024/05/14/google-search-web-filter/), the day AI Overviews began rolling out to every US user, Google added a "Web" filter. Its [AI Overviews help page](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/14901683?hl=en) says the features "cannot be turned off," but the Web filter "displays only text-based links without features like AI Overviews." In other words, Google itself ships a button that brings back the 10 blue links.

### AI answers are built from ranked results

This is the strongest evidence, and the least visible. Google's [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), updated in July 2026, says its generative features ground answers "by relying on our core Search ranking systems." It describes [query fan-out](/glossary/query-fan-out) as related queries "generated by the model to request more information and fetch additional relevant search results." OpenAI says ChatGPT search sends rewritten queries to third-party providers. Each AI answer, then, is assembled from several ranked lists that the searcher never sees. The 10 blue links became the model's reading list. Our post on [how ChatGPT search decides citations](/blog/how-chatgpt-search-decides-citations) shows what happens next on OpenAI's side.

### Clicks shrank, but didn't stop

Fewer searches end with a visit. [Pew found](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) people clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits with an AI summary, against 15% without one. [SparkToro reported](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/) that 68.01% of US Google searches ended without a click from January to April 2026, up from 60.45% in 2024, while warning that the two years come from different panels. So about a third of Google searches still send a click somewhere.

Google disputes the steep-decline story. In August 2025, its head of Search wrote that total organic click volume had been ["relatively stable year-over-year,"](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/ai-search-driving-more-queries-higher-quality-clicks/) without publishing the numbers. In its [July 2026 earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001652044/000165204426000066/googexhibit991q22026.htm), Alphabet said its "popular AI features are driving Search query growth," and Search revenue grew 17%.

| Claim                              | What the evidence says (as of September 2026)                                                     | Source                                     |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| AI answers have replaced the links | No. AI Overviews show on a minority of searches, and AI Mode handled 0.34% of them early in 2026. | Pew, 2025. SparkToro and Similarweb, 2026. |
| Google hides the links             | No. The Web filter shows links only, and AI Mode falls back to web results when unsure.           | Google help page. Google blog, 2025.       |
| Ranking no longer matters          | No. Google's AI features ground answers in its core ranking systems.                              | Google AI optimization guide, 2026.        |
| Clicks are unchanged               | No. Zero-click searches rose from about 60% to 68% in two years.                                  | SparkToro, 2026.                           |

The honest summary: the 10 blue links still exist, still feed the answers, and still send about a third of searches onward. What they lost is their monopoly on the first screen. ## Agentic Retrieval: When a Model Reads the 10 Blue Links for You

In the synthesis era so far, the searcher asks and the engine answers. In agentic retrieval, the engine reads the 10 blue links on your behalf and keeps working after the first answer. It plans several searches, reads what comes back, decides what's missing, searches again, and in some products takes an action on a site.

The vendor record shows how fast this arrived:

- **It started as research.** Google's [Gemini Deep Research](https://blog.google/products/gemini/google-gemini-deep-research/) (December 2024) drafts "a multi-step research plan for you to either revise or approve." OpenAI's [deep research](https://openai.com/index/introducing-deep-research/) followed in February 2025. In February 2026, OpenAI added the option to "restrict web searches to trusted sites" and connect deep research to apps through MCP.
- **Then it learned to act.** [Operator](https://openai.com/index/introducing-operator/) could type, click and scroll in January 2025. By July 2025, [ChatGPT agent](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-agent/) could "navigate websites, filter results, prompt you to log in securely," and asks permission first.
- **Now it runs in the background.** At I/O 2026, Google said "we're entering the era of Search agents," starting with information agents that operate "in the background, 24/7" and send a synthesized update when something changes.

Two things slow it down. Products change fast, as Atlas and Instant Checkout show. And people hesitate to hand over the final step. Our post on [conversational buyer stages](/blog/ai-search-intent-conversational-buyer-stages) covers the checkout and booking features that exist today and how few buyers trust an agent to pay.

For publishers, agentic retrieval changes who reads your page. It may be read by a model on a task, not a person skimming results. That model can also reach your data without a page at all, through connectors like MCP, which our post on [MCP as the new sitemap](/blog/mcp-protocol-new-sitemap) explains. What an agent needs from a site to finish a task is covered in [agentic SEO for autonomous AI buyers](/blog/agentic-seo-autonomous-ai-buyers).

## The 2027 Outlook: A Forecast, Not a Record

Everything above is documented. This section is not. It separates what named analysts predicted from what Rankbox expects, and it says what would prove each forecast wrong.

### What named analysts predicted, and how it's tracking

- **Gartner, February 2024.** Gartner [predicted](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-02-19-gartner-predicts-search-engine-volume-will-drop-25-percent-by-2026-due-to-ai-chatbots-and-other-virtual-agents) that "by 2026, traditional search engine volume will drop 25%," as AI chatbots become "substitute answer engines." The release doesn't describe a method. As of September 2026, we know of no public dataset that measures total search volume against Gartner's baseline. The signals that do exist point the other way for Google, which says AI features are driving query growth.
- **Semrush, June 2025.** Based on more than 500 digital marketing and SEO topics, Semrush [projected](https://www.semrush.com/blog/ai-search-seo-traffic-study/) that those topics "may start driving more visitors from AI search to websites than from traditional search by early 2028." It added that the change "could happen much sooner" if AI Mode became Google's default.

Both come from companies that sell advice or tools on this shift, so weigh them with that in mind.

### Rankbox's forecast for 2027

These are our predictions, built from the timeline above. They aren't measurements. Each carries our confidence level, and we'll mark each one right or wrong in a year.

1. **The 10 blue links survive as the fallback and proof layer.** They stay the default for navigational, local and many shopping searches, and the backup when an AI answer isn't confident. Our confidence is high. _Why:_ Google shows AI Overviews only when they are "additive," and it still ships the Web filter. _What would prove us wrong:_ Google making AI Mode the default results page for all queries.
2. **AI answers take the first screen for complex questions.** Long, multi-part questions increasingly start in AI Mode or an assistant, not a results page. Our confidence is medium. _Why:_ Google already carries follow-ups from AI Overviews into AI Mode, and it [reports](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/) AI Mode queries "more than doubling every quarter." _What would prove us wrong:_ AI Mode's share of searches flattening near today's low level.
3. **Agents become a visible share of site visits.** Background agents and browser agents fetch pages on people's behalf often enough that site owners report them as their own line in analytics. Our confidence is low to medium. _Why:_ Google, OpenAI and Perplexity, with its [Comet browser](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/introducing-comet), all ship agents that browse. _What would prove us wrong:_ agent use staying a niche for paying subscribers.
4. **Ads settle inside answers on ad-funded engines, not everywhere.** By the end of 2027, we expect labelled ads in or beside the answer to be routine in Google's AI features, Microsoft Copilot and ChatGPT's free tiers, and still absent from assistants that have ruled them out. Our confidence is medium. _Why:_ Google says ads can show ["above, below or within AI Overviews"](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16297775?hl=en), and Microsoft says its ads are ["displayed in Copilot's responses"](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/advertising/msa-help/hlp_ba_conc_adsforcopilot). ChatGPT has shown labelled ads below answers since its [US test](https://openai.com/index/testing-ads-in-chatgpt/) began in February 2026, and OpenAI now says ChatGPT Ads are [available in "over 60 countries."](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-ads-expands-southeast-asia-taiwan/) On the other side, Anthropic says ["Claude will remain ad-free,"](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-is-a-space-to-think) and Perplexity [stepped away from advertising](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/02/18/perplexity-abandons-ai-advertising/) in February 2026, according to the Financial Times. _What would prove us wrong:_ an ad-free assistant adding ads, or an ad-funded one pulling them. Our post on [ChatGPT search ads](/blog/chatgpt-search-ads-conversational-ppc) covers the formats.
5. **Ranking still decides who gets cited.** Pages that rank well for the searches an AI runs keep getting most of the citations. Our confidence is high. _Why:_ both Google and OpenAI describe answers built from search results. _What would prove us wrong:_ an engine that answers mainly from a licensed index or from model memory.

## What the Shift Means for Your Pages

This post is the thesis, not the content plan. Four things follow from it, in order.

1. **Keep ranking.** AI answers read the 10 blue links you never see. A page that ranks for the searches an AI runs has a head start on being cited. Google says optimizing for its generative features is ["still SEO"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).
2. **Compete for the sentence, not only the slot.** In the synthesis era, the prize is a mention or a citation inside the answer. That is the work of [generative engine optimization](/glossary/generative-engine-optimization).
3. **Measure each era with its own scoreboard.** Track indexing in Search Console, rankings and click-through rate for classic results, and mentions, citations and AI referrals for answers. Our guide to [measuring GEO](/blog/how-to-measure-geo) sets out the method.
4. **Make pages usable by a model on a task.** State prices, limits and next steps in plain text, so an agent or an answer can use them without guessing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are the 10 blue links dead?

No. As of September 2026, most Google searches still open on the 10 blue links, and Google's Web filter shows links only. What changed is that AI answers now sit above or instead of the links for some searches, and those answers are built from ranked results the searcher doesn't see.

### What does "10 blue links" mean?

It means the classic search results page: ten ranked links to other websites, each with a blue title. The phrase is shorthand for search as a list of places to go, as opposed to an answer written on the page. Google used it itself in a 2018 post about featured snippets.

### When did Google start moving away from 10 blue links?

In 2007. Google's Universal Search blended news, images, video and maps into the results that year. The Knowledge Graph added facts in 2012, featured snippets added extracted answers in 2014, and AI Overviews added written answers for all US users in May 2024.

### Does SEO still matter if AI answers replace blue links?

Yes. Google says its AI features rely on its core ranking systems, and ChatGPT search sends rewritten queries to search providers. Pages that rank for those searches are the pages AI answers read. SEO now also has to win the mention inside the answer, not only the position.

### What is agentic retrieval?

Agentic retrieval is when an AI model plans its own searches, reads the results, searches again and sometimes acts, instead of answering from one search. Microsoft used the term for a product in May 2025. OpenAI's deep research and ChatGPT agent and Google's Search agents all work this way.

### Will AI Mode replace the 10 blue links in 2027?

Google hasn't announced that. As of September 2026, AI Mode had passed one billion monthly users but handled a small share of searches, and AI Overviews show only when Google judges them useful. Our forecast is that classic results stay the default for simple and navigational searches through 2027.
