GEO and AEO are two names for largely the same work: being found, quoted and named in AI answers. AEO (answer engine optimization) is the older term, in print by February 2018 and born from featured snippets and voice search. GEO (generative engine optimization) was introduced in a research paper posted in November 2023 to describe AI systems that write answers from many sources. In the GEO vs AEO naming fight, neither side has won.

So the useful GEO vs AEO question isn't which term is right. It's which term your reader expects. In 2026 Google names both and files them under SEO, Bing writes "GEO" into its webmaster guidelines, and HubSpot, Forrester and Profound say "AEO." The safest habit in any formal document is to write the plain phrase, "AI search visibility," and add both acronyms in brackets.

This post covers the GEO vs AEO history: where each label started, who uses which today and why they keep blurring. For what the work itself involves, and where the two jobs really do differ, see our [complete comparison of AEO and GEO](/blog/aeo-vs-geo).

## Key Takeaways

- The earliest independent use of "answer engine optimization" found for this post is a Search Engine Watch article dated 7 February 2018. Jason Barnard says he and Trustpilot defined it in a white paper handed out in September 2017.
- "Generative engine optimization" comes from a paper by Pranjal Aggarwal and five co-authors, first posted on 16 November 2023 and accepted at KDD 2024.
- "LLMO" was in print a month before GEO: Search Engine Land used "large language model optimization (LLMO)" on 12 October 2023.
- "AIO" has meant two things. In 2023 Julia McCoy used it for humans improving AI-written content; others now use it for optimizing for AI search. "AI SEO" carries both meanings too.
- In the GEO vs AEO split of 2026, Bing and Andreessen Horowitz say GEO; HubSpot, Forrester, Profound and G2 say AEO; Google names both and calls the work SEO.
- In an RFP, job post or report, define the scope in plain words and list both acronyms, because vendors define them differently.

## Five Labels, Traced to Their Sources

### AEO: answer engine optimization

The idea of an answer engine is older than the acronym. Google's featured snippets, which it says it introduced in January 2014, reversed the usual result so the answer came first. The 7 February 2018 Search Engine Watch piece described a Trustpilot webinar with Chee Lo and Jason Barnard and said the strategy "has come to be known as AEO, or answer engine optimization." That wording suggests the label was already in use.

Barnard's site says the two defined AEO in a printed Trustpilot white paper, "The Rise of Voice and AEO: How SEO will Evolve," handed out at BrightonSEO on 15 September 2017. No copy is online, and his own page notes that an independent recap of the day confirms the stand but not the paper. Treat 2017 as his claim and February 2018 as the earliest independent record.

### GEO: generative engine optimization

GEO has a clean origin. The paper ["GEO: Generative Engine Optimization"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) by Pranjal Aggarwal, Vishvak Murahari, Tanmay Rajpurohit, Ashwin Kalyan, Karthik Narasimhan and Ameet Deshpande was posted on 16 November 2023 and accepted at KDD 2024. Its abstract calls GEO "the first novel paradigm to aid content creators in improving their content visibility in generative engine responses." It also gave the term a benchmark of test queries and a way to score visibility, which is why researchers still use it.

### LLMO and GAIO: large language model optimization

On 12 October 2023, five weeks before the GEO paper, Olaf Kopp asked in [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/large-language-model-optimization-generative-ai-outputs-433148) whether brands could influence AI output "via large language model optimization (LLMO) or generative AI optimization (GAIO)." That's the earliest LLMO use found for this post, not proof it was the first. The label stresses the model rather than the search engine, so it also covers answers written from training data alone.

### AIO: AI optimization

AIO has carried a different meaning. In [Majestic's "SEO in 2023" series](https://majestic.com/seo-in-2023/additional-insights/julia-mccoy), Julia McCoy described AIO as "taking content from AI and you're humanly optimizing it." By 2025 others used it for optimizing for AI search, as in [Search Engine Land's piece](https://searchengineland.com/origins-seo-geo-aio-462480) on "AIO" (artificial intelligence optimization) next to GEO. It also reads like shorthand for Google's AI Overviews, so spell it out if you use it.

### AI SEO

AI SEO has no single origin, because it's a plain-English phrase. It still means two things: optimizing for AI search, and doing SEO with AI tools. Search Engine Land's [guide to AI SEO](https://searchengineland.com/guide/what-is-ai-seo) uses it as the umbrella, with GEO "within AI SEO." Semrush sells its AI visibility product from an [/ai-seo/ section](https://www.semrush.com/ai-seo/overview/) of its site.

## Why the GEO vs AEO Labels Blur

Four forces keep the GEO vs AEO line fuzzy, and none is likely to go away soon.

1. **Vendors gain from new names.** Forrester's Nikhil Lai wrote that tools promoting these acronyms "tend to exaggerate SEO and AEO's differences to carve a startup-sized hole in marketers' tech stacks." A new name sells a new budget line.
2. **The machines merged.** AEO was built for answer boxes and voice assistants. Those now run on language models. Apple's [Siri AI](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/siri-ai-a-profoundly-more-capable-and-personal-assistant-is-here/), in beta from 14 September 2026, draws on "broad world knowledge" from the web. An answer engine and a generative engine are increasingly the same product.
3. **The acronyms collide.** Profound points out that GEO "conflicts with geography, geology, and geo-targeting." In local marketing, "geo" already means location. AIO collides with AI Overviews.
4. **Nobody owns a definition.** Microsoft's own guide redefines AEO as "Answer/Agentic Engine Optimization." Search Engine Land's guide notes that "the meaning of AEO has broadened to include both snippet-style visibility and AI-generated answer placement." When the meaning moves, the label stops pinning it down.

Rob Garner summed up the mood in [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/origins-seo-geo-aio-462480) in September 2025: "I personally don't think any one term will stick." He also quoted Bruce Clay, one of the people credited with naming SEO, who sees both as specialties "under the umbrella of SEO."

### Sample scope wording for an RFP

A GEO vs AEO label alone won't protect you in a contract. Spell out what success means. For example:

1. **Engines in scope:** ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Microsoft Copilot.
2. **Prompts:** a fixed panel of 40 unbranded buyer prompts, agreed in week one.
3. **Metrics:** share of answers that name us, share that cite our site, and accuracy of facts about us, each reported monthly from repeated runs.
4. **Deliverables:** page rewrites, new comparison pages and a list of third-party sites to approach.
5. **Exclusions:** paid placements, fake reviews and any tactic that breaks Google's or Bing's spam policies.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is GEO the same as AEO?

Mostly, yes. Both describe being found, quoted and named in AI answers, and many vendors use them interchangeably. They started apart: AEO with featured snippets and voice search around 2018, GEO with a 2023 paper on AI-written answers. Google treats both as SEO.

### Who coined the term GEO?

Pranjal Aggarwal, Vishvak Murahari, Tanmay Rajpurohit, Ashwin Kalyan, Karthik Narasimhan and Ameet Deshpande introduced generative engine optimization in a paper first posted on 16 November 2023. It was accepted at KDD 2024, a major data-mining conference.

### Who coined the term AEO?

No coiner is independently confirmed. The earliest independent record found is a Search Engine Watch article from 7 February 2018, which said the strategy "has come to be known as AEO." Jason Barnard says he and Trustpilot defined it in a white paper in September 2017.

### What does LLMO mean?

LLMO stands for large language model optimization: shaping how language models describe and recommend your brand. Search Engine Land used it on 12 October 2023, a month before the GEO paper. It covers the same ground as GEO, with more stress on the model than the search engine.

### Does Google use the terms AEO or GEO?

Google names both in its May 2026 guide to generative AI features but doesn't treat them as separate disciplines. It calls the work "still SEO" and warns that many "AEO/GEO hacks" aren't supported by how Google Search works, such as llms.txt files and chunking content.

### Should a job title say GEO vs AEO?

Use both, next to SEO, such as "SEO Manager, AI Search (AEO/GEO)." Candidates and recruiters search under all three labels, and the job is the same whichever acronym a company prefers. Spell out the engines and metrics in the description.
