Voice search is back because the assistants people talk to now run on large language models. Google's Search Live, Gemini Live, ChatGPT Voice, Siri AI, Alexa+ and Copilot hold a spoken conversation, search the web mid-answer and take follow-up questions. So a spoken query is no longer a short command. It's a long question that branches into a recommendation.

The numbers point the same way. In its one-year AI Mode report, Google said [the average AI Mode search is triple the length](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/ai-mode-us-insights/) of a traditional search, and its [US insights deck](https://storage.googleapis.com/gweb-uniblog-publish-prod/documents/AI-Mode-US-Insights.pdf) adds that more than one in six AI Mode searches are non-text: voice, images, video or a Live conversation. Follow-up queries grew by more than 40% a month. Those figures cover all of AI Mode in the US, not voice alone, but they describe the habits voice brings.

Most voice search advice was written for a different machine. It tells you to win a featured snippet, add FAQ markup and target "near me" phrases, because an old assistant read one short answer aloud. This post explains what changed, which assistants matter as of September 2026 and where each gets its answers, how voice prompts differ from typed ones, and a spoken-answer format you can apply to any page. For the step-by-step setup work, see our [voice search optimization checklist for 2026](/blog/voice-search-optimization-2026).

## Key Takeaways

- Voice search in 2026 runs through AI assistants that search, cite and remember context: Search Live, Gemini Live, ChatGPT Voice, Siri AI, Alexa+ and Copilot Voice.
- Gemini became the assistant on most Android phones on 4 September 2026, and Siri AI began rolling out in beta in English on 14 September 2026.
- Google says the average AI Mode search is three times as long as a traditional one, and follow-up queries grew more than 40% a month in the US.
- Every assistant here can draw on the web. Google, OpenAI, Microsoft and Amazon name the index, partners or crawlers behind it; Apple points only to its own Applebot crawl and names no search partner.
- A spoken answer has to work without a screen: one clear definition, a decisive pick with a condition, short comparable facts and an honest caveat with a date.
- Old voice tactics have expired. FAQ rich results stopped on 7 May 2026, and speakable markup still only works for US English news on Google Home devices.
- No assistant reports voice queries to site owners, so you check voice answers by asking the assistants yourself and reading the transcripts.

## Why Voice Search Is Back, and What Changed

The first voice assistants treated speech as a remote control. You said a short command or question, and the assistant ran one lookup and read one answer. That shaped a decade of voice search advice: keep answers short, win the snippet the assistant reads, and optimize for "near me."

The new assistants work more like a person on the phone. They listen while they talk, let you interrupt and keep the thread across turns. OpenAI says ChatGPT's Live voice [can listen and speak at the same time](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8400625-voice-mode-faq) and can use web search and memory. Amazon says you can [talk to Alexa+ naturally](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/alexa-plus-international-launch), change topics and skip the wake word after the first turn. Apple says users can turn [almost any Siri response into a rich conversation](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/siri-ai-a-profoundly-more-capable-and-personal-assistant-is-here/) and ask follow-up questions.

The table sums up the shift.

|                             | Old voice search (about 2014 to 2024) | AI voice search (2026)                                             |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| What you say                | A short command or question           | A full question with conditions, then follow-ups                   |
| What the assistant does     | One lookup, one answer                | Rewrites the question into several searches, then writes an answer |
| Where the answer comes from | A single result or a knowledge panel  | Several web pages, listings and partner data, combined             |
| Memory                      | None between requests                 | Earlier turns, and saved memory where the user allows it           |
| Screen                      | Often none                            | Usually a phone, with links, cards or a transcript                 |
| What wins                   | The one snippet read aloud            | Being one of the sources the answer is built from                  |

The third row is the one that matters most for your content. Google defines [query fan-out](/glossary/query-fan-out) in its [AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) as "a set of concurrent, related queries generated by the model." OpenAI says ChatGPT search [rewrites your question into one or more targeted queries](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt) for its search partners. Microsoft says Copilot sends the Bing search service a query of [a few words informed by the user's prompt](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/manage-public-web-access). A spoken question now fans out into several typed searches you never see.

### Google: Search Live and Gemini Live

Google runs two voice experiences, and they're easy to mix up. **Search Live** is voice inside Search. Google [expanded it on 26 March 2026](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-live-global-expansion/) to every language and location where AI Mode is offered. You tap Live in the Google app, ask out loud, hear an audio answer, then "continue the conversation with follow-up questions or dive deeper with helpful web links." Its [help page](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/16329036?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid) adds that you can reopen a Live session from your AI Mode history. Since mid-September it runs on [Gemini 3.8 Live](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-3-8-live-gemini-3-8-live-extended-thinking/).

**Gemini Live** is voice inside the Gemini app. Google's [help page](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15274899?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid) says it can use connected apps such as Google Flights, Hotels and Shopping, and "can automatically use public information from Google Maps in your Live chats." On answer sources, Google's [crawler documentation](https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/crawlers-fetchers/google-common-crawlers) says Gemini Apps ground answers in content "from the Google Search index." Our [Gemini guide](/ai-seo/gemini) covers what that means for citations.

Gemini now reaches far more people by voice than it did a year ago. A Google community manager wrote that on [4 September 2026 most users could no longer switch back](https://support.google.com/gemini/thread/396052272/) to Google Assistant on Android phones. On speakers, [Gemini for Home](https://support.google.com/googlehome/answer/16618650?hl=en) is in early access in 20 countries and 10 languages. Gemini Live on a speaker needs a Google Home Premium plan.

Advanced is the older real-time mode, and Standard transcribes your speech before replying. Free users get limited Live access, and Plus includes three hours in any 24-hour period.

The search side is spelled out on OpenAI's [search help page](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt): "During a voice conversation, you can ask ChatGPT to search the web." Restaurant reservation search isn't available in voice. The same page lists Microsoft among its search providers and says that to be eligible, a site should allow OAI-SearchBot. ## How Voice Search Prompts Differ From Typed Search

Put the vendor data and the research together and five differences stand out. Each one changes what a page needs before it can win a voice search answer.

### 1. Questions are longer and phrased like speech

Google's deck lists the top first words in AI Mode queries as "What," "How," "I," "Is" and "Can." People ask the question on their mind instead of guessing keywords. This isn't new to AI. Ido Guy's [2018 study of a commercial search app's logs](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a33062e7d3c52ec819bb2e53deb9cb7f2810fea3), published in ACM Transactions on Information Systems, found that spoken queries were closer to natural language than typed ones and leaned toward question answering. AI assistants simply stopped punishing that habit.

### 2. One question becomes a conversation

A typed search often ends with a click. A voice search often ends with "and what about...?" Google said in May 2025 that [Gemini Live conversations run five times longer](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/gemini-app-updates-io-2025/) than text chats on average. It gave no method, so treat that as a vendor claim. The 40% monthly growth in AI Mode follow-ups points the same way.

### 3. Context comes along for free

The listener doesn't repeat themselves, so the assistant fills the gaps. OpenAI says ChatGPT may use saved memories and rough location when it [rewrites a search query](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt): a vegan in San Francisco asking for "restaurants you might like" becomes a search for vegan restaurants in San Francisco. Our post on [how AI search uses user intent and context](/blog/how-ai-search-uses-user-intent-and-context) covers these signals in detail. For your page, it means the facts that match a condition (price for a team size, a feature for a use case) must be written down, or the assistant can't match them.

### 4. People ask for a pick, not a list

Voice users want someone to choose. Google's US data shows searches starting with "where to," "where should I" and "ideas for" growing, and planning queries growing 80% faster than AI Mode overall in six months. A list of ten links doesn't work out loud. The assistant has to name one or two options and say why. Pages that state who a product suits, and who it doesn't, give it that reason. Our model of [the four conversational buyer stages](/blog/ai-search-intent-conversational-buyer-stages) maps these turns from first question to handoff.

### 5. Names have to survive the microphone

A typed brand name arrives spelled right. A spoken one has to be recognized first. In a [SIGIR 2023 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13149), Apple researcher Christophe Van Gysel gives the example of "what is borrelia" being misheard as "what is gorilla" when the speech model rates the rare word as unlikely. Rare names are exactly what brands have. Say your product name the way customers say it, and spell it the same way everywhere, so the assistant links the spoken name to the right entity.

## The Spoken-Answer Format

When an assistant answers out loud, it reads a few sentences and moves on. Google's own guidance for its text-to-speech markup recommends [20 to 30 seconds of content, or roughly two to three sentences](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/speakable), per spoken section. That's a useful budget for any passage you want quoted in a voice search answer.

We call the structure that fits this budget the **Spoken-Answer Format**. It has four blocks and one supporting layer. You can apply it to a product page, a comparison page or a guide.

| Block                      | What it does                                       | Write it like this                                                 | Voice follow-up it answers         |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| 1. Say-it definition       | Names the thing, its category and who it's for     | One sentence, under 30 words, no pronouns                          | "What is it?"                      |
| 2. Decisive recommendation | Picks an option for a stated situation             | "If you ..., choose ..., because ..."                              | "Which one should I get?"          |
| 3. Comparison table        | Holds the facts the next questions need            | Short rows a listener would ask about: price, best for, main limit | "How much?" "Is it better than X?" |
| 4. Transparent caveat      | Says what it isn't for and when facts were checked | One or two sentences with a date                                   | "Is there a catch?"                |
| Follow-up ladder           | Answers the next three questions nearby            | One question-shaped heading each, answer first                     | "And what about ...?"              |

### Why each block earns its place

**The say-it definition** gives the assistant a sentence it can repeat word for word. Put the name and category together ("Tallyfold is an invoicing app for agencies"), so the sentence stands alone when lifted out of the page. If you need the general case for [answer-first content](/glossary/answer-first-content), our [passage-level guide](/blog/optimize-content-for-ai-search) covers it.

**The decisive recommendation** answers the question people actually ask by voice: which one? Hedged copy ("it depends on your needs") gives an assistant nothing to say. A condition plus a reason gives it a full spoken sentence and tells the listener whether it applies to them.

**The comparison table** might seem pointless in a spoken answer. It isn't. An assistant can pull a row from it when the follow-up comes, and phone assistants show results on screen: ChatGPT's Live mode can "show visual results through supported widgets." Each row should answer a likely follow-up, and each cell should make sense spoken on its own: "$60 a month for five users," not "60/5." Our [comparison page formula](/blog/comparison-page-formula) covers fair tables in depth.

**The transparent caveat** does two jobs. It tells the listener when the pick is wrong for them, which makes the recommendation believable. And a date ("prices checked on 29 September 2026") lets the assistant and the listener judge how fresh the fact is.

**The follow-up ladder** exists because voice users keep talking. After "which one," they ask "how much," "does it work with my accounting tool" and "is it cheaper than the other one." Answer each under its own question-shaped H3, with the answer in the first sentence. Our [reverse prompt playbook](/blog/reverse-prompt-playbook) has a matching kit of Answer Units for pricing and fit.

### The 30-second read-aloud test

Once a section is written, read it out loud with a timer. Score one point for each check it passes.

1. **Answer first.** The first sentence answers the question on its own.
2. **Under 30 seconds.** The answer block takes 30 seconds or less to say.
3. **No pointing.** It never says "above," "below," "this table" or "click here."
4. **Named, not implied.** The product and category are named in the answer, not referred to as "it" or "our tool."
5. **A pick with a reason.** It recommends something for a stated situation.
6. **Numbers you can hear.** Each sentence carries one or two numbers at most, with units said out loud.
7. **A dated caveat.** It says what the option isn't for and when the facts were checked.
8. **Easy to say.** A stranger can pronounce the brand name on the first try, and it's spelled the same way everywhere.

Seven or eight is ready. Five or six needs a trim. Anything lower needs the before-and-after treatment below.

## Before and After: A Tallyfold Section Rewritten for Voice

Tallyfold is a fictional invoicing and payments app for agencies. Brindlework and Kestrelyn are fictional rivals. All names, prices and features here are made up for the example.

A buyer runs a voice search in Gemini Live: "What's a good invoicing app for a five-person design agency that bills clients a monthly retainer?" Here is the section on Tallyfold's comparison page that an assistant might read.

### Before: 89 words, 1 of 8 checks

> Tallyfold is the all-in-one platform trusted by forward-thinking agencies everywhere. Our powerful features help you streamline billing, get paid faster and scale with confidence. Unlike legacy tools, we were built for the way modern teams really work. See the table below for how we compare, and check out our pricing page for plans that fit every budget. Whether you're a solo freelancer or a growing studio, Tallyfold has you covered. Ready to transform your cash flow? Start your free trial today and see why agencies love working with us.

It passes only check 8: the name is easy to say. It never says what Tallyfold is for, never picks anything and points at a table the listener can't see. An assistant can't quote a single sentence of it in answer to the buyer's question.

### After: 97 words and a table, 8 of 8 checks

> **Tallyfold is an invoicing and payments app for agencies of 2 to 50 people that bill clients on retainers or by project.**
>
> If your agency bills most clients a fixed monthly retainer, Tallyfold is the stronger pick, because it sends retainer invoices on a schedule and follows up on late payments by email. If you bill mostly by the hour, choose Brindlework, whose built-in time tracking turns hours into invoices.
>
> |                      | Tallyfold        | Brindlework            | Kestrelyn         |
> | -------------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------- |
> | Price for five users | $60 a month      | $75 a month            | $45 a month       |
> | Best for             | Retainer billing | Hourly billing         | Freelancers       |
> | Main limit           | No time tracking | No automatic reminders | One user per plan |
>
> Tallyfold doesn't run payroll or track time. Prices were checked on 29 September 2026 and can change, so confirm them on each pricing page before you buy.

### What changed, and which check each change passes

| Change                                                                                    | Check it passes         |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Opened with what Tallyfold is and who it's for, in 22 words                               | 1 Answer first, 4 Named |
| Replaced "has you covered" with a pick for retainer billing and a pick for hourly billing | 5 A pick with a reason  |
| Deleted "see the table below" and "check out our pricing page"                            | 3 No pointing           |
| Put prices in the table as spoken phrases, one per cell                                   | 6 Numbers you can hear  |
| Added what Tallyfold doesn't do and the date prices were checked                          | 7 A dated caveat        |
| Cut the claims no listener can check ("trusted by forward-thinking agencies")             | 2 Under 30 seconds      |

The arithmetic on length: the definition and the recommendation total 70 words. At an assumed speaking pace of about 150 words a minute, that's 70 ÷ 150 × 60 = 28 seconds, inside the 30-second budget. The definition alone is 22 words, or about 9 seconds. The old version ran 89 words, about 36 seconds, and said nothing a buyer could use. The table and caveat are there for the follow-ups.

Now the follow-up ladder. Under the section, Tallyfold adds three H3s the buyer is likely to ask next: "How much does Tallyfold cost for a five-person agency?", "Does Tallyfold work with our accounting software?" and "Is Tallyfold cheaper than Brindlework?" Each opens with a one-sentence answer. When the buyer says "and how much would that be for us?", the assistant has a sentence ready.

## What to Keep and Drop From Old Voice Search Advice

Most voice search guides still repeat tactics from the smart speaker era. Some still help. Some no longer do anything. Here's the sorting, with the evidence.

| Old advice                                         | Status as of September 2026                                                                                                                        | What to do instead                                               |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Win the featured snippet so the assistant reads it | Gemini replaced Google Assistant on most Android phones; Search Live speaks an AI answer built from several pages                                  | Write passages an AI answer can quote, and be one of its sources |
| Add FAQ schema for voice answers                   | Google [stopped showing FAQ rich results on 7 May 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)                                              | Keep real questions and answers as visible text                  |
| Add speakable markup                               | Still beta, and only for [US English news on Google Home devices](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/speakable)  | Use it only if you publish news in English in the US             |
| Target short "near me" phrases                     | Spoken prompts are full sentences with conditions                                                                                                  | State the conditions: price, hours, team size, fit               |
| Claim your Google Business Profile                 | Still true. Google says profiles [can help you show in AI responses](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) | Keep it, and add Apple Business and Bing Places                  |
| Treat voice as a separate channel                  | The same web index and listings feed typed and spoken answers                                                                                      | Fix the page and the listing once; both channels benefit         |

Markup built for the old assistant is going away piece by piece. Back in March 2024, Google dropped guided recipes from its docs because that Google Assistant feature [was removed](https://developers.google.com/search/updates). And Google says [structured data isn't required](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) for its AI features. Use markup for the rich results it still supports, not as a voice search switch.

Local businesses have the most to gain from the listing work, because voice search prompts about places ("a dentist near the office that's open Saturday") lean on listings. Our guide to [optimizing your business for AI search](/blog/optimize-business-for-ai-search) covers which listing feeds which engine.

## How to Check Your Voice Search Visibility

No assistant tells site owners what people asked it by voice. Search Console's [generative AI performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139?hl=en) counts impressions in AI Overviews and AI Mode, and doesn't mention Search Live or voice separately. So checking voice search is a manual job. It's easier than it sounds, because every assistant here leaves a written record.

1. **Write 10 spoken prompts.** Use full sentences with conditions, the way a buyer talks: team size, budget, location, a rival's name. Our free [AI Visibility Prompt Kit](/tools/ai-visibility-prompt-generator) gives you 30 buyer prompts to start from.
2. **Ask them out loud** in Search Live, Gemini, ChatGPT Voice and Copilot, plus Siri AI or Alexa+ if your buyers use them.
3. **Ask two follow-ups each time:** "how much would that cost for us?" and "is it better than [rival]?"
4. **Read the transcript.** Search Live keeps sessions in AI Mode history, ChatGPT adds a transcript to the chat, and Copilot shows citations in the voice transcript.
5. **Log three things:** whether you were named, whether your facts were right, and which pages or listings were cited.

Voice search answers vary from run to run, so repeat the panel monthly and read rates, not single answers. Our guide to [measuring GEO](/blog/how-to-measure-geo) explains sample sizes and a control test.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is voice search still relevant in 2026?

Yes. Google, Amazon and Apple moved their voice assistants onto large language models in 2025 and 2026, and Google says more than one in six AI Mode searches in the US now use voice, images or another non-text input. What changed is the format: people hold conversations instead of saying short commands.

### How is AI voice search different from typed search?

AI voice search uses longer, spoken questions, keeps context across follow-ups and usually ends in a recommendation. Behind the scenes the assistant turns one spoken question into several typed searches, then answers from the pages and listings it finds.

### Which voice assistants use web search?

All six assistants behind voice search today can draw on the web. Google grounds Search Live and Gemini in its Search index, ChatGPT uses its own crawler plus search partners including Microsoft, Copilot uses Bing, and Alexa+ fetches pages with Amazon's search bots. Apple says Siri AI uses the web but names no search partner.

### Does FAQ schema help voice search?

No, not as a voice signal. Google stopped showing FAQ rich results on 7 May 2026 and says its AI features need no special markup. Keep real questions and answers as visible text on the page, because assistants read the text.

### What is the best format for a voice search answer?

A spoken answer works best as one clear definition, a recommendation tied to a situation, short comparable facts and a dated caveat. Google's own text-to-speech guidance suggests about 20 to 30 seconds, or two to three sentences, per spoken section.

### How do I check if my brand shows up in voice search?

Ask the assistants yourself. Speak 10 buyer-style prompts in each assistant, add two follow-ups, and read the written transcript each one keeps. No assistant sends site owners a report of voice queries, so a monthly manual panel is the most direct check.
