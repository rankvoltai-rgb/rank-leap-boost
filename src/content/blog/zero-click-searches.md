Zero-click searches are now the normal outcome on Google. The newest large study, from SparkToro using Similarweb's panel, found that [68.01% of US Google searches ended without a click](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/) from January to April 2026. That's up from about 60% in 2024 and 49% in 2019, and the share runs higher on searches that show an AI Overview or happen in AI Mode.

A zero-click search is one where the person searches and then doesn't click any result. They may get the answer on the page, search again, or give up. Our glossary entry on [zero-click search](/glossary/zero-click-search) covers the basics. This post is about the numbers: who measured what, how, and what the figures do and don't prove.

If you want the bigger picture of what this means for a brand, read our guide to [the headless brand](/blog/headless-brand-zero-click), which covers marketing when most buyers never reach your homepage. Here we stay with the data.

## Key Takeaways

- About 68% of US Google searches were zero-click in early 2026, per SparkToro and Similarweb. The same research line measured about 49% in 2019.
- Studies count zero-click searches in different units: searches, visits, sessions or keywords. Numbers from different studies don't line up exactly.
- When Google shows an AI summary, people click a regular result about half as often: 8% of visits against 15%, in Pew's March 2025 data.
- AI Mode is close to fully zero-click. Semrush found only 6–8% of AI Mode sessions led to another website in mid-2025.
- Exposure depends on the query. Seer found AI Overviews on 36% of informational queries but only 5% of transactional ones.
- Chatbots are even less likely to send a visit. Similarweb found fewer than 7% of ChatGPT prompts included any source citation in May 2026.

## What Counts as a Zero-Click Search

Every study agrees on the basic idea: a search that ends without a click on a result. They disagree on what to count and what counts as "ending."

| Study                            | What it counts                  | What "zero-click" means there                                            | Coverage                                                   |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| SparkToro with Datos (2024)      | Individual searches             | No click on any result; includes a new search or closing the session     | US and EU, desktop and Android; no iPhones                 |
| SparkToro with Similarweb (2026) | Individual searches             | Same definition; a mobile session ends after 10 seconds without activity | US browsers on desktop and mobile; excludes the Google app |
| Pew Research Center (2025)       | Visits to a Google results page | The next page visited wasn't a result link                               | 900 US adults, one month                                   |
| Semrush AI Mode study (2025)     | Sessions                        | No visit to an outside site during the AI Mode session                   | US desktop only                                            |
| Similarweb keyword data (2026)   | One keyword's searches          | Share of that keyword's searches with no click                           | Per keyword                                                |

These choices move the numbers. A search that leads to another search counts as zero-click for SparkToro. A session in AI Mode might hold several searches. So when two zero-click searches figures differ by ten points, check the unit before you check the trend.

### Survey numbers vs. behavior numbers

One figure travels especially widely: "80% of consumers rely on zero-click results." It comes from a [Bain & Company report](https://www.bain.com/insights/goodbye-clicks-hello-ai-zero-click-search-redefines-marketing/) based on a December 2024 survey of 1,117 people. The full finding is narrower than the headline: about 80% said they rely on zero-click results in at least 40% of their searches. Bain estimated this cuts organic web traffic by 15% to 25%.

That's a useful signal of attitude, but it's what people said, not what they did. When you cite zero-click searches, prefer behavior data, name the source and date, and say which unit it counts. Here's a quick rule of thumb:

1. **Panel data** (SparkToro, Similarweb, Pew) tells you what share of searches end without a click.
2. **Keyword data** (Ahrefs, Seer) tells you how much a feature like an AI Overview changes click rates on specific terms.
3. **Surveys** (Bain) tell you how people feel about it.
4. **Your own Search Console** tells you what's happening to your site, which is the number that matters for planning.

## The Long-Run Studies: SparkToro, Datos and Similarweb

The best long-run series comes from Rand Fishkin at SparkToro. He has measured Google behavior with three different clickstream panels, which are groups of people who let a company record their browsing.

### Four readings from 2016 to 2026

1. **About 45% ten years ago.** SparkToro's 2026 report puts the 2016 figure at about 45%, from the Jumpshot panel.
2. **49% in 2019,** also from Jumpshot, which later shut down.
3. **58.5% of US searches in 2024,** and 59.7% in the EU, from [Datos' panel](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study-for-every-1000-us-google-searches-only-374-clicks-go-to-the-open-web-in-the-eu-its-360/) between September 2022 and May 2024. For every 1,000 US searches, only 360 clicks reached a site that wasn't Google's or an advertiser's.
4. **68.01% in January to April 2026,** from Similarweb's desktop and mobile panel.

The 2024 study also split what people did after a search. About 37% of searches ended the session, and about 22% led straight to another search. Almost 30% of all clicks went to Google's own properties, such as YouTube and Maps.

### Why the 2024 figure appears twice

If you read both reports, you'll see 2024 listed as 58.5% in the original and 60.45% in the 2026 update. The 2026 report doesn't explain the change. The likely reason is a different weighting, since each report blends mobile and desktop data with its own mobile share. Either way, the rise to 68% is large enough that the choice doesn't change the story.

### What the panels miss

Fishkin is open about the gaps. The 2026 data leaves out the Google search app, where Google shows even more answers on the page, so he expects the true rate is higher. The 2024 panel had almost no iPhones. And the 2026 [methodology notes](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/) say paid clicks had to be estimated from a separate benchmark. He calls the comparison across panels "apples and oranges," which is fair. Read the direction of the trend with confidence and the decimals with caution.

## What AI Overviews and AI Mode Changed

Zero-click searches were rising long before AI summaries, thanks to featured snippets, knowledge panels and instant answers. Our timeline in [The Death of 10 Blue Links](/blog/death-of-10-blue-links) dates those changes. AI features pushed the rate up on the searches they touch.

### AI Overviews

- **Pew's browsing data.** Pew tracked 68,879 Google searches by 900 US adults in March 2025. On visits with an AI summary, people [clicked a regular result 8% of the time](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), against 15% without one. They clicked a link inside the summary on 1% of visits, and ended their browsing session on 26%, against 16%.
- **Ahrefs' keyword data.** Using Search Console data for 300,000 keywords, Ahrefs found an AI Overview went with a [58% lower click-through rate](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/) for the top-ranked page in December 2025. Its April 2025 version of the study found 34.5%. The effect shrinks lower down: about 19% at position 10.
- **Seer Interactive's brand data.** Across 53 brands and 5.47 million queries, organic click rates on queries with an AI Overview [hit 1.3% in December 2025](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update), then recovered to 2.4% by February 2026. Seer says it's too early to call that a lasting rebound.

All three show that clicks drop where AI Overviews appear. None is a controlled experiment, so each shows a strong link, not proof of cause. Your click-through rate is the rate of impressions that turn into clicks; our entry on [click-through rate](/glossary/click-through-rate) explains how Search Console counts it.

### AI Mode

AI Mode, Google's chat-style search tab, goes further. Semrush studied about 69 million US desktop sessions from May to July 2025. Only [6–8% of AI Mode sessions](https://www.semrush.com/blog/google-ai-mode-seo-impact/) led to a visit to another website, so 92–94% were zero-click. Semrush notes that its figures for regular search come from separate studies with different methods.

AI Mode is still a small slice of search. SparkToro's 2026 report found only 0.34% of Google searches reached AI Mode in early 2026, and concluded it wasn't what drove the jump to 68%. It may drive the next one.

## Zero-Click Searches by Query Type: The Exposure Map

Averages hide the part you can act on. Some kinds of searches almost never send a click. Others still do. We call this table the Zero-Click Exposure Map. It sorts query types by how exposed they are, using the published data above. The examples use Tallyfold, a fictional invoicing app for agencies.

| Query type         | Tallyfold example                                    | What answers it on the page     | Evidence                                                                           | Exposure                                                                              |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Quick fact         | "net 30 meaning"                                     | Featured snippet or AI Overview | Similarweb puts "capital of Sweden" at an 85% zero-click rate                      | Very high                                                                             |
| Comparison         | "invoice vs receipt"                                 | AI Overview                     | Seer: 95.4% of informational comparison queries showed an AI Overview (267 of 280) | Very high                                                                             |
| Question           | "how do I charge late fees"                          | AI Overview                     | Pew: 60% of question-word searches got an AI summary                               | High                                                                                  |
| Local              | "bookkeeper near me"                                 | Map pack and business profiles  | Seer: 76.9% of "near me" informational queries showed an AI Overview               | High for clicks, though SparkToro still lists local businesses among search's winners |
| News               | "invoice fraud news"                                 | Top stories and AI answers      | Similarweb: zero-click news searches rose from 56% to about 69% in a year          | High                                                                                  |
| Commercial         | "best invoicing software for agencies"               | Ads, lists, some AI Overviews   | Seer: AI Overviews on about 8% of commercial queries                               | Medium                                                                                |
| Transactional      | "tallyfold pricing" or "invoice software free trial" | Ads, then the site              | Seer: AI Overviews on 5% of transactional queries                                  | Low                                                                                   |
| Branded navigation | "tallyfold login"                                    | Your own site links             | Google says branded queries usually earn higher click rates                        | Low                                                                                   |

Two patterns stand out. The more a query asks for knowledge, the more likely it ends on the results page. The more it asks to do something, such as buy, sign in or book, the more likely it still sends a click.

### Where clicks are holding up

Seer found one bright spot. On queries with no AI Overview, organic click rates rose from 2.8% in January 2025 to 3.8% by February 2026. Seer's best guess is a selection effect: the AI answers the quick questions, and the searches left over come from people who plan to click.

SparkToro's report agrees on where search still pays. It names branded searches, local businesses and high-intent transactional or tactical queries as categories that still benefit from SEO. And Google's [branded queries filter announcement](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/11/search-console-branded-filter) says branded queries "typically lead to higher-ranking pages" and higher click-through rates.

## Zero-Click Answers Beyond Google

Chat assistants have no results page, so they don't produce zero-click searches in the strict sense. They produce the same outcome more often: an answer and no visit.

- **Citations are rare.** Similarweb found that [fewer than 7% of ChatGPT prompts](https://www.similarweb.com/blog/marketing/seo/zero-click-searches/) included any source citation as of May 2026.
- **Referral traffic is small.** AI chatbots sent [0.28% of all web traffic](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-chatbot-traffic/) across 74,752 sites in March 2026, by Ahrefs' count. Google sent 28.12%.
- **News felt it first.** Similarweb found zero-click news searches on Google rose from 56% in May 2024 to [nearly 69% in May 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/02/chatgpt-referrals-to-news-sites-are-growing-but-not-enough-to-offset-search-declines/). ChatGPT referrals to news sites grew 25 times over, but from a small base.

People haven't left Google, either. SparkToro's Datos analysis from August 2025 found [95% or more of Americans](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-20-of-americans-use-ai-tools-10x-month-but-growth-is-slowing-and-traditional-search-hasnt-dipped/) still used a traditional search engine each month, even as monthly AI tool use rose from 8% to 38%. So the fall in clicks points to more answers on the page, not fewer searchers.

## How to Check Your Own Exposure

Start in Search Console's Performance report. For your top pages, compare impressions, clicks and average position over 16 months. When position holds steady but click-through rate falls, the results page is answering the query for you. Then open the [generative AI performance report](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports), which Google rolled out to all sites by 31 August 2026, to see which pages appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode.

Next, sort your queries into the rows of the exposure map above. Pages that rank for quick facts and comparisons are likely to keep losing clicks. That doesn't make them worthless; it means their value shows up as visibility, not visits. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What percentage of Google searches are zero-click?

About 68% of US Google searches ended without a click from January to April 2026, according to SparkToro's analysis of Similarweb data. The figure covers desktop and mobile browsers but not the Google app, so the true share is likely higher.

### What causes zero-click searches?

Zero-click searches happen when the results page answers the question or the person changes course. Featured snippets, knowledge panels, maps, AI Overviews and AI Mode all answer on the page. Some searches end in a new search or with the person giving up.

### Do AI Overviews increase zero-click searches?

Yes, on the searches where they appear. Pew found people clicked a regular result on 8% of visits with an AI summary, against 15% without one. Ahrefs linked AI Overviews to a 58% lower click rate for the top result in December 2025.

### Which searches are most likely to be zero-click?

Quick facts, comparisons and question-style searches are the most exposed. Among informational queries, Seer found AI Overviews on 95.4% of comparisons and 85.9% of questions. Branded and transactional searches are the least exposed.

### Are zero-click searches bad for SEO?

They cut visits, not visibility. Your page can still be the source an answer draws on, and some people who see your name search for it later. The change is in how you measure success: impressions, citations and branded searches alongside clicks.

### Is ChatGPT a zero-click search engine?

In practice, mostly yes. ChatGPT has no results page, but Similarweb found fewer than 7% of ChatGPT prompts included any source citation in May 2026. AI chatbots sent about 0.28% of web traffic in March 2026, per Ahrefs.
